## TOPIC 1: __Forensic phonetics: linguistic content determination, designing methods, and determining their reliability__
Practitioners of forensic phonetics quickly realize that general phonetics methods rarely translate directly to fieldwork. Shifting from cohort analysis to a single case study introduces numerous specific, often hidden factors. It also means moving from a controlled scientific environment to a highly complex investigative context, along with its inherent biases. 

This project evaluates current methods for transcribing low-intelligibility audio content. By reviewing and testing acoustic and perceptual techniques, it aims to offer forensic experts a scientifically grounded roadmap, even where fully calibrated solutions are not yet possible. 

Under TOPIC 1, which features several sub-projects, students will learn from a supervisor who is one of the few specialists in France called upon by the courts for criminal investigations. Students will utilize the same equipment and software as the French forensic audio department, with potential opportunities for direct collaboration.
## TOPIC 2: __Medical imaging and visual articulatory feedback for the acquisition of phonology in a foreign language__
Literature suggests that visualizing speech organs improves foreign language pronunciation, potentially bypassing the need for explicit phonetic instruction. This project investigates whether our lab's state-of-the-art technologies—ultrasound tongue imaging, electromagnetic articulography, and MRI—can help French learners master difficult English sounds (such as specific diphthongs, /l/, and /r/). We aim to determine which technology is most efficient, whether they can be integrated into classrooms, or if they can at least provide valuable pedagogical material. Conducted in collaboration with the Groupe Hospitalier Universitaire (GHU) Paris, this experimental phonetics project explores repurposing advanced medical imaging for pronunciation training and second language acquisition research. 

Selecting a project under TOPIC 2 offers the advantage of joining a [fully funded collaborative initiative](../project/sara/). Students will leverage the benefits of teamwork while securing funding for conference attendance, equipment use fees, and participant expenses.
